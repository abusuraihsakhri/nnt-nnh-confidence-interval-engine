Statistical method and interpretation

Adverse-event risk is events divided by participants. Absolute risk reduction (ARR) is control risk minus treatment risk. A positive ARR yields NNT = 1/ARR; a negative ARR yields NNH = 1/|ARR|. Zero ARR has no finite NNT or NNH. The displayed whole-person number is rounded upward.

The risk-difference interval uses Newcombe (1998), method 10: the uncorrected Wilson score intervals for independent binomial proportions are combined geometrically. When the ARR interval contains zero, reciprocation gives an unbounded/disconnected range covering benefit and harm: no finite NNT/NNH confidence interval is reported. This is not an exact binomial interval and is not suitable for matched, clustered, time-to-event, or adjusted effect estimates.

Reference: Newcombe RG. Stat Med. 1998;17:873–890.
